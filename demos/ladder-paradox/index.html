The ladder in the barn

A 10 m ladder races through an 8 m barn at 80% of the speed of light. The farmer sees a shrunken ladder and shuts both doors on it at once. The ladder sees a shrunken barn, less than half its length. Nothing breaks, and both of them are right.

What you're seeing

A ladder flies to the right through a barn with a door at each end. Each door slams shut for about one nanosecond and opens again. The farmer's two door clocks are synchronised, and both doors shut when they read 0.

In the barn's view the ladder is contracted to 6 m, so it sits inside the 8 m barn when both doors shut together. Press Ladder: now the ladder is at rest and 10 m long, and the barn rushes past contracted to 4.8 m. The ladder still gets through untouched, because in this view the doors do not shut at the same time.

Below the animation is a spacetime diagram: position across, time up. The orange band is the ladder sweeping through time, the blue band the barn. The white dots are the two door events.

The small numbers above the doors are the barn's own clocks, and those under the ladder are clocks carried on its two ends, synchronised by the ladder's crew. In the Ladder view, look at the barn clocks at one moment: they disagree. The exit clock is ahead of the entrance clock by vB/c², 21.3 ns at the default settings, so it reaches 0 first. Each side thinks the other side's clocks are badly set.

Try this

  • Slow the ladder below about 0.62c. In the barn's view it no longer fits, and both doors hit it. That is true in every view, because whether a door meets the ladder is a single event in one place.
  • Pick Midway. Now the barn and the ladder rush toward each other at the same speed, and both are contracted by the same factor, so the ladder still looks longer than the barn.
  • Drag the observer speed below zero. Now the entrance door shuts first. Which of two distant events comes first depends on who is asking.
  • Switch on the solid exit door. The ladder's front stops at the wall, but its rear keeps going until the news arrives.

With the defaults, γ = 1/√(1 − 0.8²) = 5/3. The ladder shrinks to 10 × 0.6 = 6 m and the barn to 8 × 0.6 = 4.8 m. In the ladder's frame the exit door shuts γvB/c² = (5/3)(0.8)(8 m)/c ≈ 35.6 ns before the entrance door. In that time the barn moves 0.8 × 10.7 m ≈ 8.5 m, enough for the 10 m ladder to slip through a 4.8 m barn.

Why it's strange

"The ladder is inside with both doors shut" sounds like a plain fact. It isn't. It means "the entrance shut and the exit shut at the same moment", and observers moving relative to each other disagree about which distant events happen at the same moment. That disagreement is the whole trick behind length contraction: to measure a moving object you mark both ends at the same moment, and each observer means a different set of moments.

What everyone agrees on is what happens at a single place and time: did the door meet the ladder or not? The answer is the same in every view.

Paradoxes like this were made famous by Wolfgang Rindler's 1961 paper about a fast-moving man falling through a grate, and by the pole-and-barn puzzle in Taylor and Wheeler's textbook Spacetime Physics.

The two door events are spacelike separated: light from one cannot reach the other, because they are 8 m apart and happen at once in the barn frame. For such pairs the time order itself depends on the observer. Pairs where one event could cause the other, like the ladder's front hitting a wall and its rear end stopping, always keep their order.

The solid-wall version shows there are no truly rigid objects in relativity. When the front stops, the rear cannot know until a signal travels back along the ladder, and nothing goes faster than light. Even with that signal at the speed of light, the rear moves on and the ladder ends up squashed to L√((1 − β)/(1 + β)), 3.3 m at the defaults. A real ladder's signal is a sound wave, tens of thousands of times slower, and it would be crushed long before.

The math

x′ = γ(x − vt)
t′ = γ(t − vx/c²)
γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)

The Lorentz transformation from the barn's coordinates (x, t) to those of an observer moving at v along the barn. The page builds every worldline in the barn frame as straight pieces, then applies this transformation exactly, with the observer speed slider as v, to draw the scene, the diagram and the event list. Ladder and Midway set v to the ladder's speed and to the speed at which the barn and ladder move equally fast in opposite directions.

Lbarn frame = L / γ
Bladder frame = B / γ

Length contraction, measured by marking both ends at the same moment in your own frame. With L = 10 m, B = 8 m and γ = 5/3, the ladder is 6 m in the barn frame and the barn is 4.8 m in the ladder frame, the two readouts above. Nothing is squeezed: each length is a statement about which moments count as simultaneous.

Δt′ = −γv Δx / c²

The second Lorentz line applied to two events that are simultaneous in the barn frame (Δt = 0) and a distance Δx = B apart, the two door events. In the ladder frame they are γvB/c² apart, about 35.6 ns by default, and for v > 0 the exit door goes first. The tilted dashed lines on the diagram are lines of constant t′: everything on one of them happens at the same moment for that frame.

ℓ = L √((1 − β) / (1 + β))
β = v/c

The solid-wall version. The front stops when it reaches the wall; a stop signal then runs back at the speed of light, the fastest any material could possibly react, and each part of the ladder stops when the signal reaches it. In the barn frame the rear is L/γ behind and closes on the signal at c + v, so the ladder's final length is (L/γ)/(1 + β), which simplifies to ℓ above. The ladder is trapped inside whenever ℓ < B.

Real-world applications

When "at the same moment" matters

Length contraction and the relativity of simultaneity are not just puzzles. They shape how machines are built and how clocks are set.

Particle physics

Colliding pancakes

At Brookhaven's RHIC collider, gold nuclei travel at γ ≈ 100. In the lab frame each round nucleus is flattened a hundredfold along its path into a thin disc. Physicists model the collisions as two such pancakes passing through each other, which is how the quark-gluon plasma forms.

In the demo: push the speed toward 0.99c and the moving object shrinks along its motion only.
Navigation

Synchronising GPS clocks

On a rotating Earth there is no single way to agree what time it is everywhere at once. GPS fixes "now" in a frame that does not rotate with the Earth, and receivers apply a Sagnac correction that can exceed 100 ns, about 30 m of position error if left out.

In the demo: two clocks that agree in one frame disagree in another.
Light sources

X-ray free-electron lasers

In machines like SLAC's LCLS and the European XFEL, electrons fly through a row of magnets a few centimetres apart. In the electrons' frame that row is contracted by γ, tens of thousands of times, and they wiggle fast enough to emit X-rays with wavelengths below a nanometre.

In the demo: the barn seen from the ladder is the magnet row seen from an electron.
Cosmic rays

Muons reaching the ground

Muons are made about 15 km up and most live only microseconds. From the ground, their clocks run slow. From the muon's own frame, nothing is slow: the atmosphere is contracted to a few hundred metres. Both stories predict the same number of muons at the ground.

In the demo: the barn frame and the ladder frame describe one outcome in two ways.
Electromagnetism

Magnetism in every motor

A current in a wire is electrons drifting at a fraction of a millimetre per second. For a charge moving beside the wire, the electrons and the metal ions are contracted by different tiny amounts, so the wire looks slightly charged. The resulting electric force is what we call magnetism in the lab frame.

In the demo: lengths depend on who measures them, even at everyday speeds.
Engineering

Impact testing

Strike one end of a steel bar and the far end does not move until a stress wave arrives at about 5 km/s. Engineers use this in split Hopkinson bars to measure how materials behave in crashes and explosions, timing the waves as they run along the bars.

In the demo: with the exit door shut, the rear keeps going until the news arrives.
